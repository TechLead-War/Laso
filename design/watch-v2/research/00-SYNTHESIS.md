## 1. Rules the watch imposes

Hard watchOS constraints. Any concept that violates one of these is dead on arrival.

| # | Rule | Statement | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| R1 | **Interactions are seconds, not minutes** | "performing concise app interactions that can last for less than a minute each"; "keep interactions as short as possible… then drop their wrist and move on." Measured: median smartwatch session is **exactly 5.0 s**, 142.1 sessions/day. | https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/designing-for-watchos · https://developer.apple.com/documentation/watchos-apps · https://www.kostakos.org/papers/chi17.pdf |
| R2 | **The app is not the product** | "People frequently use a watchOS app's related experiences — like complications, notifications, and Siri interactions — more than they use the app itself"; users "may never explicitly launch your app." | https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/designing-for-watchos · https://developer.apple.com/documentation/watchos-apps |
| R3 | **Only 2 families reach the Smart Stack** | `accessoryRectangular` and `accessoryCircular` only. Corner and Inline are watch-face-only. Inline gets **one tap target**. | https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/widgets · https://developer.apple.com/documentation/widgetkit/widgetfamily/accessoryinline |
| R4 | **Four independent refresh budgets** | 40–70 WidgetKit reloads/day (~15–60 min apart), min 5-minute entry spacing; **~4 background tasks/hour shared across all your complications on the active face**; ≤4 snapshots/hour; 5 BT background opportunities per 24 h. | https://developer.apple.com/documentation/widgetkit/keeping-a-widget-up-to-date · https://developer.apple.com/documentation/watchkit/wkapplicationrefreshbackgroundtask · https://developer.apple.com/documentation/watchkit/preparing-to-take-your-watchos-app-s-snapshot · https://developer.apple.com/documentation/watchkit/using-background-tasks |
| R5 | **Widgets are never real-time** | "Widgets don't support continuous, real-time updates"; "Your widget extension is not continually active, even if the widget is onscreen." Competitors ship this as user-facing docs: "Apple limits apps to only being able to update complications 4 times per hour (or every 15 minutes)." | https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/widgets · https://developer.apple.com/documentation/widgetkit/keeping-a-widget-up-to-date · https://athlytic.github.io/athlyticapp/troubleshooting/ |
| R6 | **Watch HealthKit only reaches back ~7 days** | Athlytic documents it and computes 60-day baselines on iPhone, syncing via Watch Connectivity + iCloud — and says that sync is their most common support ticket. | https://athlyticapp.helpscoutdocs.com/article/12-widget-or-complication-out-of-sync · https://athlytic.github.io/athlyticapp/troubleshooting/ |
| R7 | **Health data must be redacted in Always On** | "always hide any highly sensitive information, such as financial information or health data." Recovery scores are health data. Dim, don't delete: stroked outlines, stable layout, no sub-second motion. | https://developer.apple.com/documentation/watchos-apps/designing-your-app-for-the-always-on-state · https://developer.apple.com/documentation/swiftui/environmentvalues/isluminancereduced · https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/always-on |
| R8 | **Never fire a haptic while sampling heart rate** | "When you engage the haptic engine, HealthKit stops gathering heart rate data until after the haptic engine finishes." Also: no background haptics except during a workout session; 100 ms minimum spacing. | https://developer.apple.com/documentation/watchkit/wkinterfacedevice/play(_:) |
| R9 | **Background execution is "a few seconds", never guaranteed** | Delivery is "completely up to the system"; the system throttles "when the user is performing high-priority activities, such as exercising." Overrun → `EXC_CRASH (SIGKILL)`. Only workouts, audio and location hold a real background session. | https://developer.apple.com/documentation/watchkit/background-execution · https://developer.apple.com/documentation/watchkit/using-background-tasks |
| R10 | **Crown is navigation; crown press is reserved** | "Anchor your app's navigation to the Digital Crown." "Apps don't respond to presses on the Digital Crown." Vertical pagination beats hierarchy: "Consider limiting the content of an individual page to a single screen height." Scrolling detail views break page-based navigation. | https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/digital-crown · https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/page-controls · https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/tab-views |
| R11 | **Double Tap conflicts with lists/scroll/vertical tabs** | "Avoid setting a primary action in views with lists, scroll views, or vertical tabs." So a Double-Tap primary action and a crown-scrollable page cannot be the same screen. | https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/gestures · https://developer.apple.com/documentation/swiftui/view/handgestureshortcut(_:isenabled:) |
| R12 | **Relevance on watchOS is a different API** | `TimelineEntry.relevance` does **nothing** on Apple Watch. You need `TimelineProvider.relevance()` → `WidgetRelevance`, or `RelevanceConfiguration` (watchOS 26+). `RelevantContext.fitness(_:)` / `.sleep(_:)` map onto recovery, and each needs the matching HealthKit permission on **both** app and widget extension. | https://developer.apple.com/documentation/widgetkit/widget-suggestions-in-smart-stacks · https://developer.apple.com/documentation/relevancekit/relevantcontext |
| R13 | **No spinners** | "Avoid displaying an indeterminate progress indicator… reassure people that they'll receive a notification when the process completes." | https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/feedback |
| R14 | **Static surfaces get deleted** | "A static complication that doesn't display meaningful data may be less likely to remain in a prominent position on the watch face." Same warning for widgets. Also: one deep link per complication, never a shared one. | https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/widgets |
| R15 | **Sizes, type and touch floors** | Smart Stack widget is 152×69.5 pt (40 mm) → 191×81.5 pt (49 mm) — realistically 1 title + 2–3 body lines. Default type 16 pt, minimum 12 pt; controls 44×44 pt default, 28×28 pt minimum; complication text 10.5–19.5 pt in SF Compact Rounded; line widths ≥2 pt. Apple publishes **no** numeric watchOS safe-area insets. | https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/widgets · https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/accessibility · https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/complications · https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/layout |
| R16 | **Extended runtime is capped and single-type** | One session type per app: Self care 10 min, Mindfulness 1 h, Physical therapy 1 h, Smart alarm 30 min. Frontmost app lives ~2 min after wrist drop, 1 h maximum. | https://developer.apple.com/documentation/watchkit/using-extended-runtime-sessions · https://developer.apple.com/documentation/watchos-apps/designing-your-app-for-the-always-on-state |
| R17 | **Notifications: short look is not a channel** | "Avoid using a short look as the only way to communicate important information"; "Avoid including potentially sensitive information in the notification's title." Long look structure is fixed (sash + Dismiss); max 4 custom actions; Double Tap picks the **first nondestructive action**. | https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/notifications |
| R18 | **Colour alone can never carry meaning** | "in watchOS, the system may invert colors depending on the watch face a person chooses." Vibrant rendering mode is unsupported on watchOS; accented mode tints to the face colour. | https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/widgets |

**Consequence:** a Laso wrist score may change at most ~4×/hour and must survive being wrong-by-15-minutes relative to the phone. Bevel already carries that exact bug thread (https://feedback.bevel.health/bug-reports/p/bevel-phone-app-and-watch-app-values-dont-match).

| App | First screen | Headline number | Verdict wording | Colour language | Complication families | Repeat-open driver |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **WHOOP** | Phone only — dense scrolling home, 5 tappable widgets (Strain, Recovery, Sleep, Stress, Health Monitor) + My Day / My Plan / My Dashboard. **No watch surface at all** (band has no display, no watchOS app) | Recovery 0–100 %; Strain 0–21, non-linear (Borg) | Colour-band sentences: "well recovered and primed to perform" / "maintaining and ready to take on moderate amounts of strain" / "Rest is likely what your body needs" | Green ≥67 / Yellow 34–66 / Red ≤33 | **None** — no complications exist | Morning score ritual + Daily Outlook (incl. peer comparison) + 160+ loggable behaviours + haptic alarm that can wake you "once Whoop thinks recovery is in the green" |

| **Bevel** | Watch: scrolling dashboard mirroring the phone — "strain, recovery, and sleep as they currently stand" | Recovery, Sleep, Strain, Stress, Energy Bank — **no ranges or thresholds published anywhere** | None published | None published | 10 named (Recovery, Sleep, Strain, Stress, Energy Bank, Biological Age, Nutrition Score, Net Energy, Calories, Macros); **families unpublished**; Smart Stack unconfirmed | Smart alarm + strength-workout live sync are the only watch-native reasons to open; scores themselves are a mirror |

| **Garmin** | Glance loop, user-ordered: Body Battery = integer + multi-hour sparkline at 0 presses. | **Gentler Streak** | Watch: the **daily message** — words, no score. Manual states: Active / On a Break / Sick / Injured | One hue, two values: dark green upper, light green lower, white for your own line, orange only as a moving marker. **No red anywhere** | Not listed in their own widget docs; **unverified** | Verdict at 0 taps, prescription at 1 swipe, workout at 2 — the tightest verdict→action path found |

**Convergent pattern — what all of them do, stated plainly:**

1. **Verdict on screen one, zero taps.** Garmin glance, Gentler Streak daily message, Athlytic tiles, Oura "one big thing", Fitbit tile. Nobody makes you navigate to the answer.
2. **A number is never shipped alone.** Garmin = score + colour + word + imperative. Fitbit = score + recommendation sentence. WHOOP = colour band + sentence. Gentler Streak = sentence only. Zero apps ship a bare integer as the verdict.
3. **Explanation is progressively disclosed, never on the glance.** Garmin: level at 0 presses, causal factor list at 3. Fitbit: 2 taps to per-pillar reasoning. Athlytic: baseline HRV/RHR shown as small parenthesised numbers next to the live value.
4. **One scale, reused.** Oura's biggest structural advantage: Readiness, Sleep and Activity all use 0–100 with the same four labels. WHOOP's three incompatible scales (0–100 %, 0–21 non-linear, 0–3) is the counter-example.
5. **Cold start is a named state, not a fake number.** Garmin ships "No Status" and states 3 weeks for HRV Status. Fitbit states 7 nights. Bevel needs 2 nights. Welltory says baseline training "usually takes a week or two". Nobody invents a value.
6. **The wrist gets today; the phone gets history.** Oura buries Resilience, VO₂ Max and Cardiovascular Age in "My Health" because they are "not designed to be used every day". Training Today keeps AIS/OFI on iPhone. Athlytic computes 60-day baselines on iPhone by necessity.
7. **Watch surfaces are read-only.** Oura documents no tagging, no manual HR recording, no workout start, no real-time updates on the watch. The exceptions are all *actions the watch is uniquely good at*: Bevel's alarm and strength sets, Athlytic's live workout, Garmin's Breathwork.
8. **Colour language is small and reused.** Garmin reuses blue/orange/green/gray identically across Body Battery and Stress. Gentler Streak uses one hue at two values.

## 3. Divergent bets

| Bet | Side A | Side B | What each implies |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Frozen morning verdict vs live drifting gauge** | Fitbit: score "won't change for the remainder of the day, no matter what you do". Athlytic Recovery is "set once each morning as a readiness snapshot" | Garmin Body Battery updates all day; Ultrahuman "Dynamic Recovery is dynamic and can be improved throughout the day"; Training Today "recalculates automatically throughout the day"; Athlytic's own *Battery* updates per HRV sample | A frozen number is a **decision** (trustworthy, unambiguous, one glance/day). A live number is a **feed** (many glances/day, but it will disagree with the phone under R4/R5). Athlytic ships both and separates them by name — the cleanest resolution found. |
| **Number vs words** | WHOOP, Oura, Fitbit, Garmin Readiness, Athlytic all ship a number | Gentler Streak explicitly refuses one: "simple words, as in everyday life". Garmin Training Status is 9 named states with no number | A number is comparable and chartable but demands a mental model. Words carry judgement natively but cannot show a trend. Garmin hedges by shipping both artefacts side by side (Readiness = number, Status = words). |
| **Positional vs scalar verdict** | Everyone else: value on a 0–100 ramp | Gentler Streak Activity Path: verdict is **where your dot sits inside a band** — above = back off, inside = balanced, below = room | A single positional glyph encodes value *and* judgement together, which no score does without a second element. Cost: no comparable number, harder to speak aloud, harder to fit in `accessoryCircular`. |
| **Dense dashboard vs one big thing** | WHOOP moved *toward* density (Oct 2025: away from tabs to "a more dense, scrollable home page"); Bevel mirrors the whole phone dashboard onto the watch | Oura collapsed 5 tabs → 3 and made Today show "the most important score or insight you need right now"; Gentler Streak shows one message | Density serves power users and monetises breadth. "One big thing" serves the 5-second glance. On the watch, R1 and the 5.0 s median session say density loses. Bevel's own reviewer verdict: watch features are things "the phone app can" already do. |
| **Watch as first-class surface vs no wrist at all** | Oura, Athlytic, Bevel, Training Today, Gentler Streak all ship watch apps + complications | WHOOP ships **no display, no watchOS app, no complications** — a pure sensor plus a haptic actuator | WHOOP's bet is that the phone is where interpretation happens and the wrist only needs to *feel* things (haptic alarm on green recovery). Everyone else bets the wrist is where the daily verdict is consumed. Apple's own guidance (R2) sides against WHOOP for an Apple Watch app. |
| **Alert on absolute value vs on change** | Athlytic: Health Alerts for out-of-range metrics, Target Exertion min/max, High Stress alerts | Training Today: notify only when "average RTT changes compared to the previous four days", user-set threshold | Absolute alerts fire on states the user already knows; a reviewer explicitly complained Athlytic's stress alerts "increase anxiety" and asked for "a big friendly toggle to hush stress for a while". Delta alerts fire only on news. |
| **What the paywall guards** | Athlytic and Bevel: the score itself is behind the subscription/Pro tier | Training Today: the **number is free on the watch**; what you buy is notifications, the Smart Stack widget, Siri and the phone charts | Training Today monetises *reach without looking* — a strategy that only works if the free wrist surface is genuinely good. |
| **Where slow metrics live** | WHOOP promoted WHOOP Age / Healthspan into a dedicated Health tab | Oura demoted Resilience, VO₂ Max and Cardiovascular Age into My Health as "not designed to be used every day" | Promoting slow metrics adds perceived value per open; demoting them protects the daily loop from noise. Watch-side, R1 forces Oura's answer. |
| **Colour severity** | Garmin: full 5-step ramp including red; WHOOP: red band at ≤33 | Gentler Streak: no red anywhere, orange only as a moving marker | Red is the strongest signal available and the most anxiety-generating. Gentler Streak won a design award (self-reported) on the gentler read; Athlytic drew the anxiety complaint on the alarming one. |

## 4. Design principles for a wrist health app

Evidence strength: **A** = peer-reviewed measurement or first-party platform documentation; **B** = vendor documentation or reproducible product behaviour; **C** = single reviewer, inference, or snippet-level.

1. **Design the whole verdict to be readable in 5 seconds at 28 cm, off-axis.** Median smartwatch session is exactly 5.0 s; users take 142.1 sessions/day; the watch is held at mean 28 cm, ~50° pitch, ~10° off line-of-sight. Apple's own framing: "If you had ten seconds of someone's attention, which information would you surface?" — **A** (https://www.kostakos.org/papers/chi17.pdf · https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/wp-content/uploads/2018/08/GlanceableVis-InfoVis2018.pdf · https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2023/10138/)
2. **Ship the complication and the Smart Stack widget as the product; the app is the detail view.** Apple states users "may never explicitly launch your app". Heart Analyzer's whole value proposition is a 12-hour HRV chart *on the face*: "A glance at your watch face will provide you the weekly trend lines". — **A** (https://developer.apple.com/documentation/watchos-apps · https://www.myhealthyapple.com/monitor-hrv-apple-watch-heartanalyzer/)
3. **Never ship a bare number.** Every studied product pairs the value with words: Garmin's number + label + imperative, Fitbit's recommendation sentence, WHOOP's band sentence, Gentler Streak's message-only. — **A/B** (https://www8.garmin.com/manuals/webhelp/GUID-0221611A-992D-495E-8DED-1DD448F7A066/EN-US/GUID-C21BE0C8-A08E-4DA1-B6C6-2E0E2DDDB372.html · https://support.google.com/fitbit/answer/14236710?hl=en · https://developer.whoop.com/docs/whoop-101/)
4. **Make the words an imperative, not a description.** "Time to slow down" and "Let your body recover" tell the user what to do; "Moderate Readiness" only names a bucket. The single most consistent criticism across the competitive set is guidance, not data: "Bevel is big on data, but falls short on guidance"; Athlytic "does not tell you whether it is a steady state run or intervals"; Ultrahuman "doesn't explain why it could happen or what it could mean". — **A/B** (https://tech.yahoo.com/wearables/articles/bevel-sort-makes-apple-watch-230000160.html · https://ibikerun.substack.com/p/athlytic-app-review-iosapple-watch · https://www.garagegymreviews.com/ultrahuman-ring-review)
5. **One scale, one band vocabulary, reused across every score.** Oura: "Readiness, Activity, and Sleep Scores — and their contributors — are rated on a scale of 0-100" with the same four labels. WHOOP's three incompatible scales is the anti-case. — **B** (https://support.ouraring.com/hc/en-us/articles/360025589793-Readiness-Score · https://developer.whoop.com/docs/whoop-101/)
6. **Use bars, not rings, when a comparison must be read fast.** Measured exposure to correctly compare two values: bar 159–285 ms, donut 216–245 ms, radial bar 1548–1772 ms. Radial encodings cost 6–10× more viewing time. Apple independently prescribes a chart for `accessoryRectangular` — its exemplar is a 24-hour heart-rate graph. — **A** (https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/wp-content/uploads/2018/08/GlanceableVis-InfoVis2018.pdf · https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/complications)
7. **Show a slope, not just a level.** Body Battery's glance is "your current Body Battery level and a graph of your Body Battery level for the last several hours" — the slope answers "am I going up or down right now", which a static number cannot. AutoSleep's complication keeps updating while the user is asleep, so the 7 a.m. wrist-raise already has the answer. — **B** (https://www8.garmin.com/manuals/webhelp/GUID-0221611A-992D-495E-8DED-1DD448F7A066/EN-US/GUID-28BA6B01-9460-4FF3-8499-5D91269FD50B.html · https://autosleepapp.tantsissa.com/watch-use)
8. **Separate the frozen verdict from the live feed, and name them differently.** Athlytic ships Recovery (set once each morning) and Battery (updates per HRV sample) as two named things. A verdict that silently drifts destroys trust; a feed that never moves cannot sustain checking. — **B** (https://www.athlyticapp.com/getting-started)
9. **Borrow an existing mental model instead of teaching a new one.** Body Battery works because a phone battery already means "fills overnight, drains with use, plan your day around it". Ultrahuman justifies Movement Index by a mechanism (glucose metabolism, non-exercise energy) rather than a step count. — **B/C** (https://the5krunner.com/garmin-features/sleep/body-battery/ · https://www.ultrahuman.com/global/ring/)
10. **Progressive disclosure of "why": glance → factors → detail.** Garmin: level at 0 presses, factor list at 3. Fitbit: 2 taps to per-pillar explanations. Athlytic shows the 60-day baseline HRV/RHR as small parenthesised numbers beside the live value. Score opacity is a named product failure: "The scores are a black box". — **B** (https://www8.garmin.com/manuals/webhelp/GUID-0221611A-992D-495E-8DED-1DD448F7A066/EN-US/GUID-28BA6B01-9460-4FF3-8499-5D91269FD50B.html · https://www.dcrainmaker.com/2021/11/fitbit-readiness-review.html · https://athlytic.github.io/athlyticapp/troubleshooting/ · https://www.autonomous.ai/ourblog/bevel-app-review)
11. **Name the cold start; never fabricate a value.** Garmin ships "No Status" and requires three weeks of sleep data for HRV Status. Fitbit requires 7 nights. Oura needs 5 days for Daytime Stress and 14 nights for Cardiovascular Age. — **B** (https://www8.garmin.com/manuals/webhelp/GUID-0221611A-992D-495E-8DED-1DD448F7A066/EN-US/GUID-6F81BF5B-B49A-4506-95E2-0F4A04D8B319.html · https://support.google.com/fitbit/answer/14236710?hl=en · https://support.ouraring.com/hc/en-us/articles/21205822135315-Daytime-Stress · https://ouraring.com/blog/cardiovascular-age/)
12. **Publish the 15-minute staleness rule in-product.** Athlytic documents the 4-updates-per-hour cap as user-facing text, which pre-empts "the watch is wrong" tickets. Bevel, which does not, has a live public bug thread about watch values being 1–5 points off the phone. — **B** (https://athlytic.github.io/athlyticapp/troubleshooting/ · https://feedback.bevel.health/bug-reports/p/bevel-phone-app-and-watch-app-values-dont-match)
13. **Push the verdict at wake time; make the glance loop the follow-up.** Garmin's Morning Report arrives unprompted "based on your normal wake time", costs one press, and its section order is user-editable. WHOOP delivers recovery as a morning ritual. Oura spotlights Symptom Radar on Today "the following morning". — **B** (https://www8.garmin.com/manuals/webhelp/GUID-0221611A-992D-495E-8DED-1DD448F7A066/EN-US/GUID-4D26FDDC-63BD-4910-95B8-98937FEC2545.html · https://www.dcrainmaker.com/2021/11/whoop-platform-review.html · https://ouraring.com/blog/symptom-radar/)
14. **Give the verdict screen exactly one action, and make it the obvious next step.** Gentler Streak: verdict at 0 taps, "Go Gentler" prescription at 1 swipe, workout at 2. Garmin's Stress glance goes from "you are stressed" to a Breathwork session in one press. Waterllama's entire primary action is a Double Tap from the Smart Stack. — **B** (https://docs.gentler.app/using-gentler-streak-on-your-apple-watch/overview-of-the-apple-watch-app-interface · https://www8.garmin.com/manuals/webhelp/GUID-F41EAFB3-6CC9-42DE-9C6C-9E358DBB0671/EN-US/GUID-DD8EE575-CC72-4829-B4C8-4BE1F8D3473E.html · https://apps.apple.com/us/app/water-tracker-waterllama/id1454778585)
15. **Deep-link each complication to its own detail screen, and cap what the wrist tracks.** Apple: "Define a different deep link for each complication you support." HeartWatch treats complications as shortcuts "through to the function". Streaks caps tracking at 24 tasks, which is why one complication can represent the whole state. — **A/B** (https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/widgets · https://heartwatch.tantsissa.com/user-guide/quick-guide · https://apps.apple.com/us/app/streaks/id963034692?platform=appleWatch)
16. **Keep the colour system tiny, reused, and never load-bearing alone.** Garmin reuses blue=rest / orange=stress / green=activity / gray=unknown identically across two features; Gentler Streak uses one hue at two values with orange as the only marker. Apple requires meaning without colour because "the system may invert colors depending on the watch face". — **A/B** (https://www8.garmin.com/manuals/webhelp/GUID-0221611A-992D-495E-8DED-1DD448F7A066/EN-US/GUID-28BA6B01-9460-4FF3-8499-5D91269FD50B.html · https://docs.gentler.app/understanding-your-activity-path/interpret-the-activity-path · https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/widgets)
17. **A score that contradicts felt state is worse than no score.** DC Rainmaker on WHOOP: an 80 % recovery after 3 h 15 m of jet-lagged sleep while he "still felt like crap", and "the singular difference between a green and yellow score is simply keeping my HRV value above 54". Trust is what makes a number worth glancing at. — **B/C** (https://www.dcrainmaker.com/2021/11/whoop-platform-review.html)

## 5. Engagement mechanics ranked

Ranked by strength of the evidence that the mechanic actually works, strongest first.

| Rank | Mechanic | Evidence | Mechanism | Watch-specific implementation |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Dynamic content at near-zero access cost** | **Strong.** Checking habit = "brief, repetitive inspection of dynamic content quickly accessible on the device"; adding real-time info to a previously static screen *caused* checking to emerge. 82.3 % of watch sessions are self-initiated, 142/day. (https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00779-011-0412-2 · https://www.kostakos.org/papers/chi17.pdf) | Informational reward that is quickly accessible reinforces the check | A complication whose value visibly moves during the day — within R4's ~4 updates/hour. Static-for-23-hours scores structurally cannot sustain checking, and Apple warns static complications get removed. |
| 2 | **Surviving the first 8 days** | **Strong.** >50 % of health-app users discontinue in week 1; the sub-cohort still engaged at day 8 gained **+25 days** median retention; only ~1 in 10 become heavy users. (https://arxiv.org/pdf/1910.01165) | Early-week attrition dominates every other effect | Everything in the first week must work without a baseline: name the cold-start state (Garmin "No Status", Fitbit 7 nights), and give a useful wrist surface on day 1 rather than an empty one. |
| 3 | **Goal gradient — visible shrinking distance to a threshold** | **Strong.** 20 % interpurchase acceleration near the goal; 16 % faster completion overall; illusionary pre-stamped progress works; effort **resets after the reward is earned**. (https://home.uchicago.edu/ourminsky/Goal-Gradient_Illusionary_Goal_Progress.pdf) | Effort scales with proportion of remaining distance | A depleting gauge or a target range (Athlytic's Target Exertion Zone, Gentler Streak's Activity Path band) has a gradient; a bare score does not. Failure mode is explicit: once the goal is met, motivation collapses until a new distance is visible — so never leave the user at "done" with nothing next. |
| 4 | **Bar/donut encodings over radial** | **Strong.** 159–285 ms vs 1548–1772 ms to read a comparison. (https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/wp-content/uploads/2018/08/GlanceableVis-InfoVis2018.pdf) | Perceptual read time is the binding cost inside a 5 s session | `accessoryRectangular` bar sparkline over a decorative ring. Apple's own exemplar for that family is a 24-hour HR graph. |
| 5 | **Uncertainty about the outcome** | **Strong (mechanism), inferred (application).** Sustained anticipatory dopamine ramp scales with uncertainty, maximal at P = 0.5. (https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12649484/) | Unpredictable direction sustains anticipation; a memorised morning number does not | A wrist value the user cannot predict since last glance. Do **not** manufacture fake variance — the score must still be true, or principle 17 kills it. |
| 6 | **Push the verdict at wake time** | **Medium.** Garmin's Morning Report is a shipped, user-ordered, one-press digest; WHOOP and Oura both anchor on a morning delivery. No published effect size. (https://www8.garmin.com/manuals/webhelp/GUID-0221611A-992D-495E-8DED-1DD448F7A066/EN-US/GUID-4D26FDDC-63BD-4910-95B8-98937FEC2545.html · https://ouraring.com/blog/symptom-radar/) | Removes the fetch step from the highest-intent moment of the day | Smart Stack relevance via `RelevantContext.sleep(_:)` wakeup clue (R12), plus a wake-time notification with the verdict in the body — not a "open the app" prompt. |
| 7 | **Delta-triggered notifications** | **Strong on the near-term effect, strong that it is not retention.** Notifications give a **3.5× next-hour** lift but only **1.04–1.3× over 24 h** and **no measurable long-term retention effect**; only 9.4 % of notifications produce any session; best slot 12:30 pm, weekends stronger; no habituation measured over 30 days–12 weeks. (https://researchonline.lshtm.ac.uk/id/eprint/4670198/1/Bell-etal-2023-How-notifications-affect-engagement-with.pdf · https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6293241/ · https://www.kostakos.org/papers/chi17.pdf) | Notifications buy an open now, not a habit | Copy Training Today: fire on **change vs a 4-day average**, with a user-set threshold — not on absolute state. Budget them; a watch app living only behind pushes competes for the 17.7 % notification-initiated slice. |
| 8 | **Streak / loss-aversion mechanics** | **Medium.** Duolingo A/B: Streak Wager **+14 % D7 retention**; Weekend Amulet +4 % return, −5 % streak loss. No streak mechanic was found in any fetched Oura or WHOOP source. (https://blog.duolingo.com/how-streaks-keep-duolingo-learners-committed-to-their-language-goals/) | Loss aversion on accumulated progress | Risky for a recovery product: a streak rewards *wearing and opening*, not *recovering*. Gentler Streak's name is ironic — they ship manual "On a Break / Sick / Injured" states so the user can stop without penalty. |
| 9 | **Resumption cue (unfinished thing)** | **Mixed — one half is dead.** A 2025 meta-analysis finds **no** memory advantage for unfinished tasks (Zeigarnik fails to replicate); the **Ovsiankina** resumption effect is reliable. (https://www.nature.com/articles/s41599-025-05000-w) | Incomplete things get *resumed*, not better *remembered* | An unclosed ring or partial bar is a resumption cue on a surface the user already sees. Do not justify it as "memorable". |
| 10 | **Social / peer comparison** | **Weak.** WHOOP's Daily Outlook includes "comparisons to other members"; no effect data published anywhere fetched. (https://gadgetsandwearables.com/2025/01/24/whoop-daily-outlook/) | Normative feedback | No verified watch-side implementation exists in this research. Treat as unproven. |
| 11 | **Committed measurement session** | **Weak/structural.** Welltory's HRV measurement is a 3–5 minute Breathe session, deliberately not a glance, with a free-tier quota of one per day. (https://help.welltory.com/en/articles/4241383-taking-measurements-with-your-apple-watch) | Scarcity plus ritual | Reuse Apple's Breathe/Mindfulness app rather than building a custom timer. Only works if the measurement genuinely needs the stillness. |

**The engagement benchmark worth copying** (medium evidence, first-party): WHOOP's CPO defines engagement as wear 28/28 days + app 28/28 days + a differentiated feature ≥7 of those 28 — chosen because those correlate to retention and there is enough volume to matter (https://www.houseofkaizen.com/ama/ben-foster-whoop).

## 6. Anti-patterns — what demonstrably fails on the wrist

1. **Mirroring the phone dashboard onto the watch.** Bevel's watch app was scoped by a reviewer to three things, and its display features are things "the phone app can" already do. Density loses against a 5.0 s median session. (https://tech.yahoo.com/wearables/articles/bevel-sort-makes-apple-watch-230000160.html · https://www.kostakos.org/papers/chi17.pdf)
2. **A live-feeling number on a complication.** R4/R5 cap it at ~4 updates/hour; the visible result is the watch disagreeing with the phone. Bevel has a public bug thread for exactly this: widget values "consistently 1–5 points lower than the iPhone app". (https://feedback.bevel.health/bug-reports/p/bevel-phone-app-and-watch-app-values-dont-match · https://athlytic.github.io/athlyticapp/troubleshooting/)
3. **Absolute-threshold anxiety alerts.** Athlytic's stress notifications drew an explicit request for "a big friendly toggle to hush stress for a while" because they increase anxiety rather than help. (https://craftingworlds.com/athlytic-the-apple-watch-coach-that-actually-tells-me-what-to-do/)
4. **Black-box scores.** "The scores are a black box" — the app doesn't show "which inputs drove the number". Ultrahuman's top criticism is the same shape: it flags a temperature drop but "doesn't explain why it could happen or what it could mean". (https://www.autonomous.ai/ourblog/bevel-app-review · https://www.garagegymreviews.com/ultrahuman-ring-review)
5. **Radial and spiral chart encodings for comparisons.** 6–10× slower to read than bars or donuts — 1548–1772 ms against a 5-second budget. (https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/wp-content/uploads/2018/08/GlanceableVis-InfoVis2018.pdf)
6. **Indeterminate spinners.** Apple: "An animated indicator can make people think they need to continue paying attention to the display, which isn't a good user experience." (https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/feedback)
7. **Static complications.** Apple: a static complication "may be less likely to remain in a prominent position on the watch face"; the same warning applies to widgets. And a static score cannot form a checking habit at all. (https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/widgets · https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00779-011-0412-2)
8. **Setting a `primaryAction` on a list/scroll/vertical-tab screen.** Directly conflicts with the double-tap navigation users expect. (https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/gestures)
9. **Relying on `TimelineEntry.relevance` for Smart Stack ranking.** It does nothing on watchOS — dead code. (https://developer.apple.com/documentation/widgetkit/widget-suggestions-in-smart-stacks)
10. **Firing a haptic while reading heart rate.** HealthKit stops gathering HR data until the haptic engine finishes. (https://developer.apple.com/documentation/watchkit/wkinterfacedevice/play(_:))
11. **Showing raw health scores in Always On.** Apple's redaction rule is unconditional for health data. (https://developer.apple.com/documentation/watchos-apps/designing-your-app-for-the-always-on-state)
12. **Multiple incompatible scales.** WHOOP asks users to hold 0–100 %, a non-linear 0–21 Borg scale, and a 0–3 stress scale simultaneously — and even WHOOP's own docs and store copy disagree on whether Recovery is 0–100 or 1–99. (https://developer.whoop.com/docs/whoop-101/ · https://apps.apple.com/us/app/whoop-performance-optimization/id933944389)
13. **Inconsistent band-label copy between marketing and product.** Fitbit shipped 30 as "Low" in PR mockups and "Good" in the app, and its vocabulary drifted Low/Good/Excellent → Low/Moderate/High. (https://www.dcrainmaker.com/2021/11/fitbit-readiness-review.html)
14. **Deep hierarchy and scrolling detail views.** "If your detail views scroll, people won't be able to use vertical page-based navigation to swipe among them." Also "Minimize the depth of hierarchy". (https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/tab-views · https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/designing-for-watchos)
15. **One shared deep link for every complication.** "If all the complications you support open the same area in your app, they can seem less useful." (https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/widgets)
16. **Metaphors that break on imperfect wear.** Body Battery "resets if the watch is removed for several hours", and reviewers say it "moves for too many reasons unrelated to exercise or readiness — digestion, posture, caffeine, temperature". A depleting gauge is a trust liability the moment the user takes the watch off to charge. (https://www.androidauthority.com/garmin-body-battery-1209128/ · https://the5krunner.com/garmin-features/sleep/body-battery/)
17. **Treating notifications as a retention strategy.** Across two MRTs, time to disengagement was *not* significantly different between notification policies. (https://researchonline.lshtm.ac.uk/id/eprint/4670198/1/Bell-etal-2023-How-notifications-affect-engagement-with.pdf)
18. **A watch app that only exists behind a push.** 82.3 % of watch sessions are self-initiated and only 9.4 % of notifications yield any session — a push-only app is fighting for the 17.7 % slice. (https://www.kostakos.org/papers/chi17.pdf)

## 7. Open questions / unverified

**Platform (Apple):**
- Apple publishes **no numeric watchOS safe-area insets** and **no Smart Stack line count**; the point-value screen table in apple-hig.md is px÷2 arithmetic, not Apple's. Smart Stack size tables have **no rows for 42 mm or 46 mm** (Series 10/11) — untested on real hardware.
- Four Apple articles 404'd: "Create accessible experiences for watchOS", "Supporting multiple watch sizes", "Making background requests", "Enabling the double-tap gesture". **No published watchOS network throttling numbers were found.**
- The `digitalCrownRotation` overload carrying haptic-detent parameters could not be resolved; its signature is deliberately absent.
- Wrist flick (watchOS 26) has no HIG page or third-party API surface found — appears system-handled only.
- Apple's HIG complications page returned only a title on one fetch path; part of that guidance reaches the welltory-heart.md report second-hand via https://significa.co/blog/designing-better-ux-for-apple-watch.

**Competitors:**
- **No competitor publishes its complication families.** Athlytic, Bevel and Training Today all describe complications without naming watchOS families; Athlytic's official complications list URL 404s and its own two store pages conflict (8+ vs 15+). Oura names shapes (circular/rectangular/corner) but not families. Gentler Streak's watch complications are not in their own docs.
- **WHOOP's own rationale for home-screen ordering is unverified** — support.whoop.com and whoop.com/thelocker return 403. WHOOP Sleep Performance bands, Stress 0–3 labels, the push-notification catalogue, the Pace of Aging −1x…3x range and the iOS widget metric list are all snippet-level or third-party.
- **Bevel publishes no score ranges, no band thresholds, and no notification inventory.** Its Smart Stack support could not be confirmed either way — treat as absent.
- **Training Today's traffic-light colour thresholds are undocumented.** Whether its dynamic structured workouts render on the watch is unverified.
- **Ultrahuman's entire watchOS screen list and complication set is unverified** — no official doc found across three searches. Its factor weights, caffeine half-life constants and cut-off times are unpublished.
- **Welltory's Energy/Stress numeric ranges, colour thresholds and Productivity formula are unverified** — the help pages deliberately omit them.
- **Oura's watch tab order is unverified** (DC Rainmaker lists Readiness → Sleep → Activity; the support doc names no order). Its Resilience 5-label list is snippet-level.
- **Garmin:** the Connect IQ glance *design guidance* page is JS-rendered and was never read. No factory-default glance order could be verified. Sleep-score band labels and stress band ranges are snippet-level, not from a fetched manual page. The ~190×63 px glance canvas and 32 kB memory limit are a community developer's experimental figures, not a Garmin spec.
- **Fitbit publishes no colour language for Readiness.** Tile mechanics (8-tile cap, reordering) are snippet-level.
- **Gentler Streak's "Go / Take it easy / Rest" label set is NOT verified** and must not be quoted. Its 2024 Apple Design Award (Social Impact) is self-reported on its own store listing; Apple's Watch App of the Year editorial URL 404'd.
- **Direct contradiction, unresolved:** Garmin's manual says caffeine and food have *no* impact on Body Battery; the5krunner says caffeine moves it. One is wrong; the research could not adjudicate.

**Evidence gaps:**
- **The central "depleting gauge drives more checking than a static score" claim has no direct empirical test.** It is an inference assembled from three peer-reviewed results (checking habits need dynamic content; uncertainty sustains anticipation; goal gradient). Treat it as well-reasoned, not measured.
- **No user-behaviour data exists for Body Battery.** Garmin publishes population averages only (avg Body Battery 71), never view counts.
- **No watchOS-specific retention benchmark exists** from any credible source found; the D1/D7/D30 numbers in engagement-science.md are marketing-vendor blogs, directional only.
- **No Oura engagement/retention numbers exist.** **No WHOOP churn/retention percentages are published** — only the CPO's 28/28/7 engagement definition.
- The Apple Watch "60–80 checks/day" figure is 2015 self-report and the source page 403'd; the defensible number is 142.1 sessions/day from CHI 2017 telemetry.
- Gartner's "30 % abandon within 6 months" was not retrieved at source. Duolingo "2.4× retention" claims are marketing blogs only — unusable.
- Oulasvirta 2012, Loewenstein 1994 and the 2025 Zeigarnik/Ovsiankina meta-analysis are **abstract/snippet-level only** (ACM, Springer and Nature all blocked full text). The Pizza et al. video-study numbers are cited secondhand through Blascheck et al.
- **Reddit was hard-blocked to every research agent.** There is zero forum-level user commentary in this entire research set — the "why people love Body Battery" evidence is thinner than it should be.
