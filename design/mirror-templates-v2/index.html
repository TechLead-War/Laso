Ten ways to write a number
on your own face.

That is what the capture screen offers today. Every template is the same idea moved to a different corner: a score, a date, a badge. Nothing there is worth keeping, and nothing there is worth showing anyone.

The photo is the most personal thing this app will ever hold. This is a proposal for what we put on it instead.

What we ship today

All ten, drawn at the real size

These are the current overlays, rendered exactly as MirrorOverlay.swift draws them. Seen together the problem is obvious: nine chips and an empty one. Change the score in the bar above and watch how little moves.

Two of them are worse than plain. Heartbeat draws an ECG trace that is a fixed decorative polyline, the same nine points on everyone, every day. Glow paints a coloured border and calls it a score. And whichever one gets picked is burned into the file forever, so a photo taken today can never wear a template we write tomorrow.

The thing under the templates

Readable is a mechanism, not a mood

There are only five ways to make type survive on top of a photo, and every template is a choice among them. Today we use one of them, always the same one, at a fixed strength, and we hope. On a backlit window selfie the hope runs out.

How the engine decides

Before the overlay is drawn, we already know two things about the photo that we currently throw away: where the face is, and how bright each part of the frame is. Vision gives us the first for free. A sixteen cell luminance sample gives us the second in under a millisecond.

The renderer then walks up the ladder on the left until the text block it is about to draw clears contrast. Most photos stop at step one. Nothing else in this proposal matters as much as this, because it is what makes every template below safe to ship.
