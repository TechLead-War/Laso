Curiosity, Personal, Proof for every screen. Blue ring = my pick. Each card has the marketing reason, the psychology reason, the risk, real sources, and a Strong/Good/Weak/Risky badge.

My review (before yours)

Strong, coherent funnel built on one honest spine: every screen earns the user's Vitality Age from their own data, then the paywall sells keeping a number they already own. The mixed-variant strategy is right, not lazy, because the job of each screen genuinely changes: Curiosity to create forward pull on momentum/gate screens, Personal to build ownership on input and payoff screens, Proof to calm and de-risk on the scary vital-sign reveals (heart, HRV). Tone is mostly on-brand: calm, plain, second-person, privacy kept backstage, no privacy-as-hero. The biggest real risks are not the copy quality, they are three structural things: a Curiosity open-loop pattern that repeats often enough to start feeling like a tic, a sign-in hard gate stacked immediately before the paywall, and a couple of Proof variants leaning on social-proof and geo claims (thousands your age, Indian HRV norms) that are likely unverifiable and clash with the otherwise honest funnel. There is also one literal copy defect (screen 11 Proof). Fix those and this is a confident ship; the per-screen picks are about 85 percent right, with three I would change.

Strongest

  • Screen 9 Health permission, Curiosity pick: this is the one true hesitation gate and 'your real age is hidden, only your data knows it, connect and reveal' is the single strongest pull in the whole funnel, framing permission as the gate to the prize rather than a privacy chore.
  • Screen 11 Verdict, Personal pick: 'You called it' paying off a prediction the user pre-committed to on screen 6 is the highest-trust moment in the flow, commitment-and-consistency done cleanly, the chart carries the proof and the warmth lands for an anxious user.
  • Screen 6 Prediction, Personal pick: pre-registering a falsifiable claim from the user's own stated symptoms ('we are calling it, see if we are right') is a genuine trust device, not a sales line, and it sets up the screen 11 payoff. This prediction-to-verdict arc is the structural backbone of the funnel.
  • Screen 12 Heart and Screen 14 HRV, Proof picks: putting cardiologist authority and gentle peer-belonging exactly on the two scariest vital-sign reveals is the correct emotional move, it converts a potential worry-spiral into a credible discovery for a 35 to 50 heart-anxious user.
  • Screen 17 Paywall, Personal pick: 'Your Vitality Age is yours now, keep it moving' reframes the trial as keeping an earned number rather than buying a new thing, the most honest and lowest-fear path to a paid yes, and it cleanly recaps the value just felt.

Weakest / risky

  • Screen 11 Verdict, Proof variant has a literal copy defect: the subhead reads 'On s your sleep runs shorter' which is broken text. Must be fixed even though Proof is not the pick here, since variants get swapped in tests.
  • Screen 17 Paywall, Proof variant makes two soft-fraud-risk claims: 'Indian HRV norms' (a geo assumption that appears nowhere else in the funnel and may not match the user) and 'Thousands your age already track theirs here' (invented social proof). On the payment screen these are the highest-liability lines in the set. If the numbers are not real, cut them.
  • Curiosity on emotional-peak or payoff screens is correctly flagged Risky in the digest (prediction, wearable, heart, hrv) and the copy confirms why: withholding meaning on a vital-sign reveal ('steady tells only half the story') can read as teasing an anxious user about their own heart, which is the exact wrong feeling at that moment.
  • Screen 16 Sign in is a hard gate sitting immediately before the paywall, screen 17. Even the assembled Funnel file's own consistency critic flags this as stacked friction and suggests moving sign-in to after the trial starts. Two consecutive commitment asks (sign in, then pay) right at the end is the most likely single drop-off point in the funnel.
  • Screen 5 Symptoms, Proof variant ('the early signs heart specialists look for first') risks tipping a health-anxious user toward fear by framing benign symptoms as clinical red flags right before a reveal. The digest correctly does not pick it, but it is the riskiest single variant for the target persona.

Cross-funnel concerns

  • Curiosity open-loop repetition: the 'we will show you next / see what it reveals / find out' device fires on screens 5, 7, 9, 10, 13, 15, 16 and 17 across the picks. Used 2 to 3 times it is propulsion; used this often the user starts to feel withheld-from on every tap, which erodes trust for someone already anxious. Thin the open loops so each one feels like a real reveal, not a habitual stall.
  • Earned-number repetition: 'your own data, not a guess / not an average / not a survey' appears on nearly every Personal and Proof screen (6, 8, 10, 11, 12, 13, 14, 17). It is the right spine but the identical phrasing repeats enough to read as a script. Vary the wording so the honesty does not become wallpaper.
  • Stacked friction at the close: connect-Health (9), sign-in (16), paywall (17) are three commitment asks in the back third. The connect ask is well-motivated, but sign-in before paywall is the avoidable one. Resequence so the user hits at most one hard gate before the trial.
  • Tone drift toward clinical on Proof: several Proof variants (5, 12, 14) lean on 'cardiologist / heart specialist' language that, for the 35 to 50 heart-conscious user, can read as a doctor's-office warning rather than reassurance. Proof works when it calms (heart, hrv) and misfires when it diagnoses (symptoms). Keep Proof to the reveal screens, not the input screens.
  • Prediction-verdict overlap: screen 6 Personal ('we are calling it / see if we are right') and screen 11 Personal ('you called it') are the same beat twice. It is intentional and mostly works, but check that screen 6 does not over-promise a binary yes/no that the real data might not cleanly deliver, or the screen 11 payoff feels rigged.

Picks I would change

  • Screen 5 Symptoms: change pick from Curiosity to Personal. Curiosity's CTA 'See what they add up to' adds yet another open loop in a stretch already dense with them (screens 5, 7, 9, 10), and Personal's 'each one you pick teaches your number what to watch for in you' is just as forward-pulling while building ownership and staying calmer for an anxious user. This also breaks up the Curiosity cluster.
  • Screen 16 Sign in: keep Curiosity copy if sign-in stays, but the stronger move is to change the structural pick by moving sign-in to after the trial starts, as the assembled funnel's own critic recommends. If it must stay pre-paywall, Personal ('This number is yours, keep it') is a safer pick than Curiosity's loss-aversion 'Don't lose the number you just found', which manufactures anxiety about losing a number one tap before asking for money.
  • Screen 13 Sleep: reconsider Curiosity in favor of Proof. Curiosity's 'what that missing hour is doing to you, we will show you next' dangles an unnamed health consequence at a heart-anxious user, which is the riskiest framing of a sleep shortfall. Proof states the same honest gap (6.2 vs 7 to 8) against a credited standard so it lands as fixable rather than ominous, and it keeps Proof consistent across the reveal block (12, 13, 14) instead of breaking the calm-authority tone for one screen.
