The 30-Day Transformation

  The honest starting point: Your user bought an Apple Watch. It's been collecting data for months, maybe years. That data sits in Apple Health — a
  graveyard of numbers nobody looks at. The watch is essentially a notification device that also counts steps.

  Your user isn't lazy. They care about their health. They just don't know what to do with 43 metrics and no interpretation.

  ---
  Day 0: "I wonder how I'm actually doing."

  They download Laso. Two screens, they're in. They see a number — say, 72. For the first time, their health has a shape. Not "10,247 steps" or "7h 12m
  sleep" — a single honest answer to "how am I doing?"

  And more importantly, they see where the 72 comes from. Sleep is dragging them down. Heart health is strong. Activity is mediocre.

  The shift: From drowning in data to seeing yourself clearly.

  ---
  Day 7: The first weekly review.

  They open the app — not because it nagged them, but because it's Monday and they're curious. The weekly review says:

  - Score 72 → 74
  - Win: Sleep duration up 11%
  - Watch out: Steps dropped 8%

  That's it. No lecture. No 15-minute reading session. Just: here's what got better, here's what slipped. Ten seconds, they're done.

  But something happened in that ten seconds. They felt the connection between sleeping earlier on Tuesday and Thursday, and the score moving. Nobody told
  them to sleep earlier. They just did, and the app noticed.

  The shift: From "I have numbers" to "I see cause and effect in my own body."

  ---
  Day 14: The quiet insight.

  By now a pattern surfaces that no generic health article could have told them. Maybe it's: "Your resting heart rate is 6 bpm lower on mornings after you
  walked more than 8,000 steps." Or: "Your deep sleep drops when your exercise minutes are above 60."

  This is their pattern. Personal. Specific. The kind of thing that would take a sports physiologist weeks of observation to notice — and it just appeared
  on their screen.

  They didn't go looking for it. The app surfaced it because the data was already there, waiting to be read.

  The shift: From generic health advice to "I know something specific about my body that I didn't know two weeks ago."

  ---
  Day 21: The invisible habit.

  Something subtle has happened. They glance at their recovery in the morning. It's low. They don't cancel their workout — they just do an easier one. They
  don't think of this as "using a health app." It's more like checking the weather before deciding what to wear.

  The app hasn't asked anything of them. No streaks to maintain. No badges to earn. No guilt. It just made their existing data legible, and they naturally
  started reading it.

  The shift: From "data I ignore" to "awareness that quietly shapes my day."

  ---
  Day 30: The real transformation.

  Their score went from 72 to 78. Not because they trained for a marathon or overhauled their diet. Because they:
  - Noticed sleep mattered most (the app showed them)
  - Saw which nights were bad (the app correlated it)
  - Made two small adjustments (they decided themselves)
  - Watched the number move (the app confirmed it)

  Here's what they now have that they didn't on Day 0:

  1. A baseline. They know what "normal" looks like for their body — not for a 35-year-old male average, but for them.
  2. An early warning system. If their resting HR trends up for a week, they'll see it before it becomes a doctor visit.
  3. Personal cause-and-effect knowledge. Not "exercise is good for you" — but "when I walk after dinner, my next-day HRV improves by 8ms."
  4. Quiet confidence. The vague anxiety of "am I healthy?" is replaced with "I know where I stand and what moves the needle."

  ---
  What this is NOT

  - Not "I became a biohacker." Your audience doesn't want that.
  - Not "I check my phone 20 times a day." They check less, but each glance means something.
  - Not "I hit a goal someone else set." There are no goals. Just their own trajectory.
  - Not "I was motivated by the app." They were informed by it. Motivation came from seeing their own data prove that small changes work.

  ---
  The one sentence

  In 30 days, Laso turns a passive wrist device into a quiet understanding of your own body — so you know what's normal, what's changing, and what actually
  works for you.

  The transformation isn't behavioral. It's perceptual. They see their health differently. The behavior follows on its own.

  ---
  This has product implications for what you build next. The app should never feel demanding. Every screen should answer a question the user already had,
  not create a new obligation. The premium feeling isn't in animations or gradients — it's in respecting their time and intelligence.


  Their #1 thing to fix — The single weakest area holding their score down. Not five suggestions. One.


================================================================================
NETWORK OFF / LOW BANDWIDTH — What happens
================================================================================

Core data (HealthKit) — FULLY OFFLINE
  - All health data is read from the on-device HealthKit store. Zero network needed.
  - Live vitals streaming, activity rings, sleep, readiness score, insights, correlations,
    trend analysis — all work with no internet.
  - HealthKit anchored object queries and observer queries continue in background.

Firebase Remote Config — GRACEFUL FALLBACK
  - On first launch with no network: all 13 feature flags, pricing, limits, and system
    config use hardcoded in-app defaults (RemoteConfigManager.defaults).
  - After a successful fetch, Firebase SDK caches values locally. Subsequent offline
    launches use the cached config. Production fetch interval: 1 hour.
  - fetchError is set but never blocks UI — the app continues with last-known or default values.

Firebase Analytics — QUEUED OFFLINE
  - Events are queued locally when offline and flushed when connectivity returns.
  - No data loss. Firebase SDK handles this automatically.

StoreKit 2 (Subscriptions) — PARTIAL
  - Subscription status is cached on-device by StoreKit. isPro / trial status works offline.
  - New purchases require network. Purchase buttons will fail gracefully with StoreKit errors.
  - Transaction listener picks up completed transactions when network returns.

iCloud Key-Value Store (PersistenceManager) — EVENTUAL SYNC
  - Baselines and preferences are saved to UserDefaults locally first.
  - iCloud KVS syncs when network is available. NSUbiquitousKeyValueStore handles this.
  - Cross-device sync is delayed but no data is lost.

Push Notifications — DELAYED
  - Local notifications (watch reminders, alerts) work fully offline.
  - Remote push delivery is delayed until connectivity returns.

What does NOT work offline:
  - Export to web report (ChartJSBundle loads CDN resources — has offline bundle fallback)
  - First-time Remote Config fetch (falls back to defaults)
  - New subscription purchases
  - iCloud sync to other devices

Low bandwidth:
  - App is essentially unaffected. The only network calls are Firebase config fetch (~2 KB),
    analytics event flush (~small batches), and StoreKit. No images, no API calls, no streaming.
  - Firebase SDK has built-in retry with exponential backoff.

================================================================================
MISSING SENSOR / NO WATCH — Current App Behavior
================================================================================

When a watch doesn't have a particular sensor, or when the user doesn't own
a watch at all, HealthKit simply returns no samples for that metric.

Current behavior per metric when data is nil/missing:

  Live Tab (LiveView):
    Heart Rate        → Shows "Syncing" placeholder, no BPM displayed
    SpO2 / Resp Rate  → Shows "Syncing" in the vital card
    HR Zone            → Defaults to "Rest" zone, zone bar at 0%
    Session stats      → Min/Avg/Max row hidden entirely
    Today's HR range   → Row hidden entirely
    Activity rings     → Shows 0/goal for all rings (or "No activity yet" before 10 AM)
    Blood Pressure     → Card hidden entirely (only shows when data exists)
    Body Temperature   → Card hidden entirely (only shows when data exists)
    Last Workout       → Card hidden entirely if no recent workout
    All vitals stale   → "Wear Your Apple Watch" prompt replaces hero card

  Home Tab (HomeView):
    Readiness Score    → Recovery card hidden when score is nil (needs both RHR + HRV)
    Readiness stale    → "Recovery Unavailable" card shown when data > 24h old
    Stress Level       → Shows "No Data" when HRV or RHR is nil
    Sleep              → Sleep card / insights hidden when no sleep data
    Smart Action       → Hidden when no real data backs it

  Vital Status labels:
    .unknown           → "No Data" (gray dot)
    .normal            → "Normal" (green dot)
    .elevated          → "Elevated" (orange dot)
    .low               → "Low" (yellow dot)
    .critical          → "Critical" (red dot)

  Dashboard / Insights:
    - Analysis engine skips metrics with insufficient data points
    - Baselines require minimum 7 days of data to compute
    - Correlations require both metrics to have overlapping data
    - Insights are only generated for metrics with enough history

What this means per watch type:

  Apple Watch (all sensors)
    → Full experience. All metrics, live streaming, readiness, everything works.

  Garmin / Huawei / COROS / Suunto / Polar / Oura / Whoop (most sensors)
    → Near-full experience. HRV, sleep stages, SpO2 all sync. Missing: live
      streaming (data arrives in batches via companion app, not real-time).
      Live tab may show "Waiting for data" more often.

  Noise / boAt / Fire-Boltt (basic sensors)
    → Limited experience. Gets: HR, steps, SpO2, basic sleep duration, calories.
      Missing: HRV, sleep stages, respiratory rate, wrist temperature.
      Impact: No readiness score (needs HRV + RHR), no stress level, no sleep
      stage breakdown, simpler insights.

  Fitbit / Samsung / Amazfit / Xiaomi / Google Pixel Watch (mid-range sync)
    → Good experience. HR, sleep stages, steps, SpO2, workouts.
      Some may lack HRV depending on model/app version.

  iPhone only (no watch)
    → Minimal: steps, distance, flights climbed, walking speed.
      No heart rate, sleep, SpO2, readiness, or live vitals.
      Home tab shows data but most cards are hidden. Live tab shows
      "Waiting for Live Data" empty state.

  No device at all
    → Empty state on Home tab: "Connect Your Health Data" with setup guide.
      Live tab: "Waiting for Live Data" with tips to pair a watch.

Critical metrics and their fallback:
  - Readiness Score: requires BOTH HRV + RHR. If either missing → score is nil, card hidden.
  - Stress Level: same as readiness — derived from HRV + RHR.
  - Sleep Quality: requires sleep samples. No samples → sleep section hidden.
  - Heart Rate Zone: requires HR. No HR → defaults to "Rest" zone.
  - Activity Rings: always shown (defaults to 0/goal), since Apple Health
    always has step/calorie data even from iPhone alone.
